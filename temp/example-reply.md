Yes—Carlson already has a general [PhD FAQ](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/faq), along with pages for [IDS program information](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/concentrations/information-decision-sciences) and [admission requirements and deadlines](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/admissions/requirements-deadlines). I would use these as the first-line response rather than write a duplicate FAQ immediately.

However, the existing pages do not fully answer the question prospective students are usually really asking: “Is this kind of PhD appropriate for my goals and background?” A short IDS-specific “Before You Apply” page or standard email would be valuable.

My assessment, with high confidence:

* Do not tell this person whether they are likely to be admitted based only on this biography.
* Explain that an IDS PhD is research training, not primarily an advanced professional credential or a way to integrate prior professional experience.
* A law/MBA/IT/project-management background does not disqualify them. The important questions are research interests, quantitative preparation, evidence of academic potential, and understanding of an academic research career.
* Do not automatically offer an open-ended meeting to every inquiry. Ask them first to review the materials and send a CV plus a short description of possible research interests. If there appears to be reasonable alignment, offer a brief 20-minute conversation.
* Avoid encouraging them to contact multiple faculty members before they can articulate a research interest.

A suitable reply would be:

> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for reaching out and for sharing your background. Your path across law, business, information technology, and project management is certainly distinctive. Students can enter Information Systems doctoral programs from many academic and professional backgrounds, so having a nontraditional background does not by itself prevent someone from being a viable applicant.
>
> The most important consideration is whether the program fits your goals. The IDS PhD is a full-time, research-intensive program designed primarily to prepare students to conduct original research and pursue academic careers. Admission therefore depends not only on professional experience, but also on research interests, academic and quantitative preparation, letters of recommendation, test scores, and the overall strength and fit of the application. We would not be able to assess admission likelihood from a brief description alone.
>
> As a first step, I encourage you to review the following resources:
>
> * [IDS PhD program overview](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/concentrations/information-decision-sciences)
> * [Admissions requirements and deadlines](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/admissions/requirements-deadlines)
> * [PhD frequently asked questions](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/faq)
> * [IDS faculty and their research areas](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/graduate/phd/concentrations/information-decision-sciences/faculty)
>
> After reviewing these materials, you are welcome to send me your résumé or CV and a short paragraph addressing two questions: What kinds of research problems would you like to study, and what do you hope to do professionally after completing the PhD? That information would help me offer more useful guidance about whether the program appears aligned with your goals. If helpful after that, we can arrange a brief conversation.
>
> Best,
> De Liu
> PhD Coordinator, Information and Decision Sciences

For recurring inquiries, I suggest creating a one-page “Considering the IDS PhD?” resource covering:

1. What the PhD is—and is not.
2. Expected career outcomes.
3. Full-time commitment and typical duration.
4. Whether a specific prior major is required.
5. Quantitative and research preparation.
6. How research fit is evaluated.
7. Whether applicants need to identify an adviser beforehand.
8. Funding.
9. Whether the coordinator can pre-assess someone’s admission chances.
10. What to send before requesting an individual meeting.

That page would complement the official admissions FAQ instead of repeating administrative information. Until it exists, the response above can serve as your standard template.
